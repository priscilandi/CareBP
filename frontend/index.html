How to measure your blood pressure

  • Before measuring your blood pressure, sit quietly for 5 minutes. Avoid caffeine, smoking, and exercise for at least 30 minutes beforehand.
  • Sit with your back supported and both feet flat on the floor.
  • Rest your arm on a table, this will keep the cuff at heart level.
  • The cuff should fit securely against your skin without being too tight or too loose.
  • Stay still and don't talk while your blood pressure is being measured.

Select your blood pressure range

Blood pressure is recorded as systolic (top number) over diastolic (bottom number), pressure is measured in mmHg. These categories apply to adults who are not pregnant.

If your systolic and diastolic readings fall into two different categories, use the higher category. If your systolic blood pressure is 180 or higher or your diastolic blood pressure is 120 or higher, wait one minute and check it again. If either number remains that high, contact a healthcare professional immediately. Call 911 if you experience symptoms such as chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, or vision changes.

Learn about blood pressure

Short, easy-to-understand answers. Tap any topic to learn more.

How to manage Blood Pressure? You can manage high blood pressure by adopting a heart-healthy diet, cutting back on sodium, and staying physically active. It is also important to maintain a healthy weight, reduce stress, get enough sleep, and avoid alcohol and tobacco. Monitoring your blood pressure numbers and taking prescribed medications exactly as directed by your doctor are vital steps.

For more information, visit the American Heart Association website.

What is high Blood Pressure? High blood pressure, also called hypertension, occurs when blood pushes against the walls of your arteries with consistently high force. It affects nearly half of U.S. adults and is sometimes called a “silent killer” because it often has no noticeable symptoms.

For more information, visit the American Heart Association website.

What do the numbers mean? Blood pressure is measured using two numbers: Systolic pressure (top number) and Diastolic pressure (bottom number). According to The American Heart Association, these readings can be grouped into five distinct categories: Normal, Elevated, Stage 1 Hypertension, Stage 2 Hypertension, and Hypertensive Crisis.

For more information, visit the American Heart Association website.

Risk of high Blood Pressure There are many factors that can contribute to the risk of developing high blood pressure. Knowing your risk factors will help you be more aware and stay healthier.

For more information, visit the American Heart Association website.

What are the Signs and Symptoms of high Blood Pressure? High blood pressure is often called the “silent killer” because it usually has no noticeable symptoms. It can affect people of all ages and may damage the heart, blood vessels, kidneys, and other organs over time if left untreated.

For more information, visit the American Heart Association website.

Health Threats from High Blood Pressure Uncontrolled high blood pressure can damage blood vessels and reduce blood flow to important organs over time. This can increase the risk of serious health problems, including heart attack, stroke, heart failure, vision loss, and problems with memory and thinking. If you have high blood pressure or are at risk of developing it, taking steps to manage your blood pressure can help protect your health.

For more information, visit the American Heart Association website.

About CareBP

A community tool to help people understand their blood pressure and find places to get blood pressure checked.

Education and navigation, not diagnosis

CareBP gives general, easy to understand information based on the blood pressure range you select. It doesn't diagnose any condition or replace a conversation with a healthcare provider. If you're having a medical emergency, call 911.

Your Privacy

CareBP doesn't ask for your name, exact blood pressure numbers, or any personal health information. Your exact blood pressure numbers are not requested or stored. Your selected blood pressure category ID is sent to the API to retrieve guidance. ZIP codes and optional search terms are also sent to the search API. These requests may appear in standard server logs.

Built for the community

This tool is designed with rural communities and women's health in mind — groups who often face extra barriers to blood pressure screening and care.

Growing over time

CareBP starts with blood pressure. Future versions may expand to cover other everyday health topics important to your community.
