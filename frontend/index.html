How to measure your blood pressure correctly

  • Sit quietly for 5 minutes first - avoid caffeine, smoking, or exercise for 30 minutes beforehand.
  • Sit with your back supported and both feet flat on the floor.
  • Rest your arm on a table so the cuff sits level with your heart.
  • Wrap the cuff snugly on a bare arm, not over clothing.
  • Stay still and avoid talking while the reading is taken.

Select your blood pressure range

Shown as systolic / diastolic (mmHg). Adult ranges, not for pregnancy. Choose the higher category if your upper and lower readings differ. If a reading is above 180 systolic or 120 diastolic, repeat after a minute and contact your clinician immediately if it stays that high call 911.

How to manage Blood Pressure? You can manage high blood pressure by adopting a heart-healthy diet, cutting back on sodium, and staying physically active. It is also important to maintain a healthy weight, reduce stress, get enough sleep, and avoid alcohol and tobacco. Monitored numbers and taking prescribed medications exactly as directed by your doctor are vital steps.

For more details visit American Heart Association

What is high Blood Pressure? High blood pressure, or hypertension, happens when the force of your blood pushing against your artery walls is consistently to high. What do the numbers mean? Blood pressure is measured using two numbers: Systolic pressure (top number) and Diastolic pressure (bottom number). According to The American Heart Association, these reading can be group into five distinct categories: Normal, Elevated, Stage 1 Hypertension, Stage 2 Hypertension, and Hypertensive Crisis.

For more details visit American Heart Association

Risk of high Blood Pressure There are many factors that can contribute to the risk of being diagnosed with high Blood Pressure. Knowing tour risk factors can help you be more aware of the likelihood of being diagnosed, according to the American Heart Association, the risk factors can be categorized as either Modifiable (can change) or Non-Modifiable (cannot change).

For more details visit American Heart Association

What are the Signs and Symptoms of high Blood Pressure? High Blood Pressure is often called the "silent killer" because for most people, it has no signs or symptoms. It affects all age groups from children to elders and can secretly damage your body over the course of years without making you physically sick.

For more details visit American Heart Association

Health Threats from High Blood Pressure Uncontrolled high Blood Pressure progressively damages the body by narrowing, stiffening, or rupturing blood vessels, which restricts vital blood and oxygen flow to vital organs. This sustained vascular strain can lead to severe medical complications acress multiple organ systems, including heart attack, stroke, heart failure, vision loss, and cognitive decline. If you have high blood pressure or are predisposed to high blood pressure, it is important to make changes to reduce the risk of major health problems and complications.

For more details visit American Heart Association

How can you check at home? Sit still for five minutes first, keep your feet flat and arm supported at heart height, and take two readings a minute apart. Some community locations and pharmacies may have screening equipment; check access before visiting.
Pregnancy and blood pressure Blood pressure can rise during pregnancy in ways that need closer watching than usual. Regular checks throughout pregnancy help catch changes early for you and your baby.
Midlife and menopause changes Blood pressure often rises after menopause as hormone levels shift. It's a normal reason to start checking more regularly, even if your numbers were always fine before.
Talking to your provider Bring your range (not just one reading) and any questions you have. It's okay to ask what a number means or what to watch for — that's what the visit is for.

About CareBP

A community tool to help people understand their blood pressure and find real places to get checked and cared for.

Education and navigation, not diagnosis

CareBP gives general, plain-language information based on the range you select. It doesn't diagnose any condition or replace a conversation with a healthcare provider. If you're having a medical emergency, call 911.

Your Privacy

CareBP doesn't ask for your name, exact blood pressure numbers, or any personal health information. Your exact BP numbers are not requested or stored. Your selected BP category ID is sent to the API to retrieve guidance. ZIP codes and optional search terms are also sent to the search API. These requests may appear in standard server logs.

Built for the community

This tool is designed with rural communities and women's health in mind — groups who often face extra barriers to blood pressure screening and care.

Growing over time

CareBP starts with blood pressure. Future versions may expand to cover other everyday health topics important to your community.
